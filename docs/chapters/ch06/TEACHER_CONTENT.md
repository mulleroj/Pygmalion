# Chapter VI Teacher Content — Her Own Voice

Status: human-reviewed Chapter VI teacher content, locked for future S01 implementation. Teacher Mode is read-only, non-scoring and non-mutating. It must not record student choices, signals, challenge answers, completion or replay history. Chapter VI has five scenes and no moral ranking of endings.

## 1. Chapter Overview

After the reception, Eliza considers what comes next and recognizes that new linguistic tools have expanded her repertoire without erasing her earlier voice. She chooses one of three equally legitimate personal directions, practises contextual inference, chooses how to frame her own statement, and reviews a descriptive account of her learning.

## 2. Learning Goals

- distinguish accent from intelligence and social worth;
- identify likely addressee and communicative purpose from context;
- understand register as a repertoire chosen in context;
- discuss language and power without treating adaptation as submission;
- reflect on agency, economic independence and identity;
- compare rhetorical purposes without ranking declaration, reflection or commitment.

## 3. Language Focus

Vocabulary: `voice`, `choice`, `work`, `housing`, `community`, `proposal`, `colleague`, `private`, `organise`, `decide`.

Functional language: asking permission to address a group; arranging a shared task; requesting a private conversation; describing future plans. Focus on how address, pronouns, purpose and setting help listeners interpret a message.

## 4. Listening Focus

LC15 checks contextual inference, not accent recognition or identity. Three short samples express a related underlying intention across public, familiar-colleague and private-adviser contexts. Learners infer likely addressee and purpose. Every item has replay and a penalty-free equivalent contextual text alternative. Transcript availability must not expose a pending answer. The objective key belongs only in Teacher Mode.

## 5. Key Vocabulary

`repertoire`, `register`, `addressee`, `purpose`, `formal`, `familiar`, `private`, `independence`, `agency`, `identity`.

## 6. Cultural and Literary Context

Discuss how social expectations, gender and economic access shape who is heard and who receives credit. The chapter is an original educational ending inspired primarily by Shaw's *Pygmalion*. It does not reproduce a musical ending, dialogue, song, costume identity or staging from *My Fair Lady*. The thematic comparison remains at the level supported by the existing project documentation: language, class, opportunity and agency.

## 7. Scene Notes

### S01 — The Morning After

Focus: opportunity versus dependency; practical conditions such as pay, housing and working arrangements; who gets to define success. Higgins tends to frame outcomes through method but does not own Eliza's achievement. Pickering is supportive and recognizes that he has sometimes spoken for Eliza. Mrs Pearce focuses on practical needs and asks what Eliza wants. `next_contact` changes only who speaks first; all routes converge with equal information. No answer key.

### S02 — The Question in the Mirror

Focus: self-recognition, code-switching, register, identity and accent prejudice. More than one voice can belong to one person. Optional earlier-scene replay is read-only; there is no identity quiz or answer key.

### S03 — Three Ways Forward

**D12:** personal direction choice. It has **no answer key**. `social_success` (learner-facing **PUBLIC PARTICIPATION**), `independent_voice` and `integrated_identity` are equally legitimate. Do not ask students to identify the “best” future or infer personality from a selection.

**LC15 answer key (objective pragmatic/context inference):**

| Sample | Correct contextual inference | Audible/contextual evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `lc15_sample_public` | `lc15_public_organiser` — meeting organiser; permission to present a proposal to the group | Direct address to “Chair,” projected public delivery, asks to explain an idea. |
| `lc15_sample_colleague` | `lc15_familiar_colleague` — familiar colleague; arrange a shared practical task | First name, “we,” and “after lunch.” |
| `lc15_sample_private` | `lc15_private_adviser` — trusted adviser; private advice on a first step | Named trusted addressee, request for privacy and advice-seeking wording. |

The key describes a likely contextual fit, not a universal rule. Accent and prestige are not evidence. Discuss how listeners can revise an inference if new context appears. Retries/support carry no penalty.

### S04 — Her Own Statement

Focus: rhetorical purpose and self-definition. `final_statement_shape` is a rhetorical preference with **no answer key**. Declaration, reflection and commitment do not indicate confidence, courage or moral quality. Rendering the composed statement awards nothing. After the learner explicitly confirms/delivers it, `ch06_final_statement_delivered` awards `Confidence +1` exactly once for Eliza speaking for herself; it is not tied to a shape and is not a student grade. Replay, reload, Teacher Mode, Back/Forward and viewport changes do not award it.

### S05 — The Voice She Chooses

Focus: agency, economic independence, identity and narrative reflection. The three presentations have no rank. All branches use the same final line, “I have more ways to speak, and the choice is mine,” before the descriptive summary. The summary describes only available saved history, does not invent missing details, and never shows numeric development signals as quality scores. Read-only replay cannot mutate progress. Explicit Finish leads to the final book-complete screen; no Chapter VII is implied.

## 8. Discussion Questions

- When can changing register help a person reach a goal?
- What is the difference between adapting to a context and letting someone else define you?
- How can people keep several ways of speaking without treating one as false?
- What practical conditions make a choice genuinely available?
- Why might a speaker want to ask privately, publicly or with a colleague?
- How can someone give credit without speaking over the person who did the work?

## 9. Sensitive Framing

Never describe Cockney as unintelligent, broken or an earlier self that must be discarded. Never describe cultivated speech as inherently superior or false. Do not imply that Public Participation means higher status, class assimilation, or abandoning community. Do not imply that work is less prestigious than public recognition. Eliza owns her choices; Higgins does not certify her worth.

## 10. Suggested Classroom Use

Use the three LC15 samples to identify address and purpose, then ask learners to name the contextual clue supporting each inference. For D12 and statement shape, invite optional reflection without collecting, grading or comparing personal answers. Discuss material conditions alongside language choices. Suggested session: 20–30 minutes of reading/replay and 15 minutes of discussion.
