# Chapter V Teacher Content — The Reception

Status: content locked for planning. Teacher Mode is contextual support; its preview is read-only and never applies rewards, choices, challenge answers, audio playback or progression.

## 1. Chapter Overview

At a flower-growers' exhibition in Lambeth Public Rooms, Eliza uses the language skills she has developed while deciding how she wants to use them. The chapter examines audience, social inference, authorship and future agency. It is not another test of whether she can imitate a class.

## 2. Learning Goals

- Match register to audience and purpose without treating one voice as superior.
- Infer relationship, purpose and formality from spoken and situational context.
- Ask for practical information about a future choice.
- Recognise assistance and shared work without giving another person ownership of Eliza's achievement.
- Identify more than one legitimate next step.

## 3. Language Focus

Vocabulary: exhibition, growers, display, programme, introduction, credit, work, future, contact.

Focus on audience awareness, requests, active/passive framing, future questions and code-switching as a communicative repertoire.

## 4. Listening Focus

LC13 contrasts professional coordination, a socially distant guest's compliment/request and a familiar colleague's practical request. Learners use relationship and purpose as context; accent is not a proxy for intelligence, truth or status.

LC14 asks for a question that fits a particular information need and listener. Many phrasings may work. Its key is contextual, not a universal ranking of direct, indirect, formal or informal speech.

## 5. Key Vocabulary

exhibition, audience, programme, growers, introduction, locally grown, credit, contribution, possibility, introduction, arrange, contact.

## 6. Cultural / Literary Context

Lambeth Public Rooms and this event are original elements of this adaptation. The scene is not a ballroom or a recreation of a musical/film sequence. Discussion may consider Edwardian public events, work, gender and class, without presenting a prestigious variety as inherently more intelligent or morally superior.

## 7. Decisions — Teacher Notes

- **D10** stores a register plan. Tailoring by role, listening then adjusting, and keeping a core voice with pragmatic changes are equally legitimate.
- **D11** stores a response to the social framing of Eliza's work. Accepting it temporarily, redirecting credit publicly and choosing a private conversation are all valid; delay is not submission.
- **future_question_style** is a personal expression choice, not a scored decision.
- **next_contact** informs Chapter VI entry/context only. It does not remove an ending or punish the learner.
- The S02 first-responder choice is temporary and has no later consequence.
- The chapter's only development-signal change is the unconditional Independence +1 on explicit S05 completion. No personal choice or challenge answer receives a player-facing moral judgement.

### LC14

- **Pickering branch:** “Could you tell me which introductions would actually help?” fits his stated point about money and introductions. The programme question and speech-opinion question do not ask for that clarification.
- **Mrs Pearce branch:** “Which should I sort out first, and what can I do myself?” fits the two practical matters she names. The flower question and broad question about what people expect do not establish a practical priority.

The answer key measures fit to the authored context. It does not establish that other real-world ways of speaking are wrong. Replay, transcript support and retry carry no penalty.

## 9. Discussion Questions

- How can Eliza change a register while keeping her own voice?
- Who should receive credit for work done by Eliza and the growers?
- When might someone choose to answer a difficult question later or in private?
- What practical information could help someone make a decision about their future?
- Which different next steps remain open at the end of this chapter?

## 10. Sensitive Framing

Keep “Accent ≠ intelligence” explicit. Register is not personal worth; code-switching is repertoire, not identity replacement. Do not praise a “proper” voice, label a working-class voice as deficient, or imply that a public confrontation is morally stronger than privacy or delay. Higgins remains self-assured and analytical, not a caricature. Pickering's partial correction retains his established respect without making him flawless. Mrs Pearce offers practical support without deciding for Eliza.

## 11. Suggested Classroom Use

Compare the three LC13 contexts, then discuss how audience and purpose shape language. Invite students to compare D10 and D11 strategies without ranking personal choices. Use LC14 to distinguish a question that fits the immediate need from one that asks a different, still legitimate question. Do not score identity or accent.
